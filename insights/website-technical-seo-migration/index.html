Website Migration SEO Checklist: Move Domains or Platforms Without Losing Control

Plan a website migration with URL mapping, redirects, canonical tags, analytics, content checks and post-launch monitoring.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

A website migration can involve a domain change, a new CMS, a URL structure update, a redesign or a move to a different hosting environment. Each change can affect crawling, indexing, analytics and user journeys. Planning the move carefully is safer than trying to repair missing pages after launch.

Define the migration scope

List exactly what is changing: domain, protocol, subdomain, CMS, URL paths, design, content or hosting. Multiple simultaneous changes make problems harder to diagnose. Record the business reason and identify stakeholders responsible for SEO, development, analytics and approvals.

Crawl and document the existing site

Export current URLs, titles, meta descriptions, canonical tags, status codes, internal links and important traffic or conversion data. Identify pages with backlinks, enquiries or strong organic visibility. This becomes the baseline for the new site.

Create a one-to-one URL map

Map each old URL to the most relevant new destination. Preserve URLs where possible. Use permanent redirects for pages that have genuinely moved. Do not redirect every retired page to the homepage; unrelated redirects can confuse users and search engines.

Prepare the staging site carefully

Use a protected staging environment and prevent accidental indexing where appropriate. Test navigation, metadata, canonical tags, structured data, robots directives, forms and mobile layout. Confirm that production settings will be correct at launch.

Plan analytics and conversion tracking

Record existing measurement settings and test tags, events, consent behaviour and campaign parameters on the new site. Confirm that form submissions and purchases are still recorded. Keep the old analytics baseline available for comparison.

Launch with a technical checklist

Verify DNS, HTTPS, redirects, sitemap, robots.txt, canonical URLs, internal links, 404 handling and key page responses. Check that important old URLs resolve to the intended new pages. Submit the updated sitemap in Search Console.

Monitor after launch

Watch Search Console indexing and crawl reports, server errors, analytics, rankings and lead volume. Compare the same page groups with the baseline. Investigate sudden changes systematically rather than changing many things at once.

Keep a rollback and ownership plan

Retain backups and a documented recovery plan. Make sure the business controls its domain, hosting, CMS administrator accounts and analytics properties. Record who can approve urgent fixes.

Key takeaway

A safe migration begins with a complete URL inventory and ends with measured post-launch checks. Keep redirects relevant, preserve important content and monitor indexing and conversions after the change.
