LMS Features Checklist: What to Evaluate Before Choosing a Learning Platform

Compare learning management system features for course delivery, learner experience, assessments, reporting, integrations, security and support.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

A learning management system should support the way an organisation teaches, assesses and supports learners. A long feature list does not guarantee a good fit. Start with the learning model and operational requirements, then evaluate how each platform handles the full learner journey from enrolment to completion.

Map the learning model

Clarify whether courses are self-paced, instructor-led, cohort-based, blended or delivered through live sessions. Identify learner groups, expected enrolment, languages, course formats and whether external customers or internal staff will use the platform.

Evaluate course authoring and delivery

Check how lessons, video, documents, downloadable resources and learning paths are organised. Confirm mobile usability, progress tracking and how updates to course material are managed. Test the experience using a real sample course.

Assess quizzes and completion rules

Review question types, grading, retakes, pass marks, certificates and assignment workflows. Confirm whether completion rules support the intended learning outcomes and whether instructors can review learner work efficiently.

Review roles and administration

Define permissions for learners, instructors, course creators, managers and administrators. Check bulk enrolment, group management, approvals, support workflows and audit logs. Avoid giving every staff member administrator access.

Check integrations and payments

List required integrations such as video conferencing, CRM, email, payment gateways, single sign-on and analytics. Confirm which integrations are native, require third-party tools or need custom development. Review transaction fees and subscription limits.

Review reporting and data portability

Check reports for enrolment, progress, assessment results, completion and learner engagement. Ask how data can be exported and what happens if the organisation changes platforms. Confirm ownership and retention terms for learner records.

Evaluate security, accessibility and support

Review authentication, backups, privacy controls, data location, incident support and accessibility. Check service availability, support response times, onboarding and training. Ask for a clear explanation of responsibilities between vendor and customer.

Run a pilot before committing

Test a representative course with actual learners and instructors. Record issues with navigation, mobile access, support, reporting and administration. Compare total cost over the expected usage period, not just the entry-level subscription.

Key takeaway

Choose an LMS by testing the complete learning and administration journey. Course delivery, assessment, permissions, integrations, reporting, accessibility and long-term data access should all be part of the decision.
