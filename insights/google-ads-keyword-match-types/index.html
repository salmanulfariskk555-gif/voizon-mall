Google Ads Keyword Match Types: How Broad, Phrase and Exact Match Work

Understand how Google Ads keyword match types affect search queries, reach, relevance, negative keywords and campaign optimisation.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

Keyword match types help advertisers control how closely a user's search needs to relate to a keyword before an ad may be eligible to show. They are not a substitute for understanding intent. The right setup depends on the offer, conversion data, budget and the amount of control the advertiser needs.

Start with the search intent

Before choosing a match type, write down the problem the customer is trying to solve. A person searching for a service price may be comparing providers, while someone searching for a definition may only be learning. Separate research queries from high-intent commercial queries when the campaign objective requires it.

Broad match

Broad match can reach searches related to the keyword, including variations and contextually related queries. It may help discover new demand when conversion tracking and smart bidding signals are reliable. It can also spend on irrelevant searches if account structure, landing pages or conversion goals are weak. Review search terms and use suitable negative keywords.

Phrase match

Phrase match can show ads on searches that include the meaning of the keyword, with additional words or variations around it. It offers a middle ground between reach and control. Do not assume that the exact words must appear in the same order; consult current Google Ads documentation because matching behaviour evolves.

Exact match

Exact match is intended for searches with the same meaning or intent as the keyword. It can provide tighter relevance, but it does not mean only one literal spelling will trigger an ad. Close variants and meaning-based matching may apply.

Use negative keywords carefully

Negative keywords help exclude searches that are clearly unrelated, such as job seekers when advertising a paid service. Review search terms before adding exclusions. Overly broad negative lists can block valuable searches, and negative keyword matching rules differ from positive keyword matching.

Structure and measure

Group keywords by closely related intent and send each group to a relevant landing page. Measure qualified leads or sales, not clicks alone. Review search terms, conversion quality, cost per qualified action and lost impression share before making large bid or match-type changes.

A safe testing process

Change one meaningful variable at a time where possible. Compare performance over a suitable period and account for conversion delay. If broad match is tested, use accurate conversion goals, adequate data and a budget that can support learning. Do not judge a test after a handful of clicks.

Key takeaway

Match types are tools for managing reach and intent, not fixed guarantees about exact wording. Build campaigns around customer intent, relevant landing pages, accurate conversion tracking and regular search-term reviews.
