Email Deliverability Basics: SPF, DKIM, DMARC and Sender Reputation

Understand email authentication and list practices that help legitimate business messages reach inboxes reliably.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

Email deliverability is affected by sender authentication, reputation, recipient expectations, message quality and technical configuration. A campaign can be beautifully designed and still perform poorly if messages are sent to people who did not expect them or the domain is not configured correctly.

Authenticate the sending domain

SPF identifies which services are authorised to send for a domain. DKIM adds a cryptographic signature that receiving systems can verify. DMARC tells receiving systems how to handle messages that fail alignment checks and provides reporting options. Configure records with the domain administrator and the sending provider's current instructions.

Understand alignment and testing

A record existing in DNS does not guarantee that authentication is correct. Check SPF lookup limits, DKIM selectors, DMARC alignment and the actual headers of test messages. Avoid publishing multiple conflicting SPF records.

Build a permission-based list

Send marketing email to people who have an appropriate expectation and permission to receive it. Do not buy lists or scrape addresses. Make unsubscribe options clear and process opt-outs promptly.

Maintain list hygiene

Remove invalid addresses, monitor hard bounces and investigate spam complaints. Do not repeatedly send to inactive contacts without a re-engagement plan. Keep suppression lists so unsubscribed recipients are not accidentally re-added.

Set realistic sending practices

Warm up new sending infrastructure carefully where relevant, send consistent volumes and avoid sudden large blasts to old lists. Use a recognisable sender name and subject line that accurately describes the message.

Monitor reputation and engagement

Review delivery errors, bounce rates, complaints, unsubscribes and engagement trends. Low opens alone can be affected by privacy features and should not be treated as a perfect measure of reading.

Follow current provider requirements

Mailbox provider requirements change. Review the latest guidance from major providers and your email service. Keep authentication and unsubscribe mechanisms working as the sending programme grows.

Key takeaway

Reliable email delivery combines domain authentication, permission-based lists, good sending practices and ongoing monitoring. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC correctly, then protect sender reputation through relevant communication.
