How to Write an Influencer Campaign Brief: A Practical Brand Checklist

Learn what to include in an influencer brief: objectives, audience, key messages, deliverables, disclosure, timelines, approvals and measurement.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

A clear influencer campaign brief reduces avoidable revisions and helps a creator produce content that serves the campaign without losing their own voice. It should explain the business problem and the non-negotiable facts, not dictate every word of the final post.

1. Campaign background and objective

Explain the product or service, why the campaign is happening now and what the audience should understand or do. Choose one primary objective such as awareness, product education, event attendance, enquiries or sales. Define what success will mean before contacting creators.

2. Audience and market context

Describe the intended audience in plain language: location, language, needs, familiarity with the category and common objections. Include relevant context such as whether the campaign is local, regional or national. Avoid vague descriptions such as 'everyone'.

3. Key message and factual proof

Write the main message in one or two sentences. Provide accurate product details, eligibility conditions, pricing if approved, service limitations and evidence for any performance claim. List claims the creator must not make. Never ask a creator to invent a personal experience or guarantee an outcome.

4. Deliverables and publishing details

Specify the number and type of posts, video length range if relevant, story frames, platform, tags, links, hashtags, publishing window and whether cross-posting is included. Clarify whether raw files, captions, thumbnails or analytics screenshots are required.

5. Creative freedom and brand safety

Share visual references and tone guidance, but allow the creator to use language that feels natural to their audience. Identify sensitive topics, competitor restrictions and any safety or legal considerations. Agree how factual corrections will be handled.

6. Disclosure, approvals and responsibilities

State the required advertising disclosure and who checks it. ASCI guidance says material connections such as payment, gifts or affiliate benefits need clear disclosure. Set a realistic review process, response time and a named contact. Do not remove disclosure to make a post look less promotional.

7. Usage rights and paid amplification

A creator's post does not automatically give the brand unlimited rights to reuse it. Specify where the content may be used, for how long, in which territories, whether editing is allowed and whether paid amplification or whitelisting is included. Agree fees and permissions separately.

8. Timeline, budget and reporting

List briefing, draft, review, publishing and reporting dates. Confirm fee, payment milestones, cancellation terms and what happens if a post is delayed. Define metrics such as reach, views, engagement, link clicks, qualified leads or purchases and agree the reporting window.

Key takeaway

A good brief is specific about goals, facts, disclosures, deliverables and rights while leaving creative execution to the creator. Written expectations protect both sides and make campaign results easier to evaluate.
