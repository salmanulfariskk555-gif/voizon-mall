Choosing a Learning Management System: Questions to Ask Before You Build or Buy

Compare LMS requirements for learners, instructors, courses, assessments, reporting, payments and ongoing support.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

A learning management system should fit the way an education provider teaches, manages learners and measures progress. Choosing a platform based only on a feature list can lead to unnecessary cost or a system that staff and learners struggle to use.

Map the learning model

Clarify whether courses are self-paced, instructor-led, cohort-based or blended. Identify learner types, expected enrolment, languages, accessibility needs and whether the platform serves one organisation or multiple clients.

List essential learner and instructor workflows

Consider registration, enrolment, course access, video lessons, downloads, quizzes, assignments, certificates, instructor feedback and learner support. Draw the journey from sign-up to course completion before selecting features.

Evaluate reporting and administration

Decide what administrators need to see: enrolment, attendance, completion, assessment results, payment status and engagement. Check whether reports can be exported and whether staff permissions can be separated by role.

Check integrations and payments

Review payment gateways, email, CRM, video conferencing, analytics and identity integrations. Confirm regional payment support, transaction fees, refunds and how access is granted or revoked after payment.

Plan privacy, security and accessibility

Understand what learner data is collected, where it is stored, who can access it and how long it is retained. Review backups, updates, accessibility, mobile usability and support arrangements.

Compare total cost and future flexibility

Include hosting, licences, custom development, integrations, maintenance, support and content production. Run a pilot with real instructors and learners before a full launch. Explore web application development with VOIZON.

Estimate the total cost of ownership

Compare licence fees, active-user limits, storage, video hosting, payment processing, integrations, onboarding, customisation, support and future migration. Confirm whether pricing is based on registered users, active learners, courses or administrators.

Test the learner and instructor experience

Ask a small group to complete a real lesson, quiz and certificate workflow. Have instructors upload content, review submissions and run reports. Note navigation issues, mobile usability and the effort required for routine administration.

Plan data protection and exit options

Review where learner data is stored, who can access it, how backups work and how records can be exported. Understand what happens to courses and learner history if the subscription ends. Keep a written responsibility matrix for the vendor and institution.

Frequently asked question

Should an organisation build or buy an LMS?

Compare the cost and maintenance burden of custom development with the flexibility and limitations of existing platforms. Build custom only when important requirements cannot be met reliably by a suitable product.
