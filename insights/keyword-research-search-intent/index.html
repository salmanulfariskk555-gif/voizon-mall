Keyword Research and Search Intent: Choose Topics Your Customers Actually Need

A practical approach to keyword research, search intent, topic selection and mapping search terms to website pages.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

Keyword research is not about collecting the largest possible list of search phrases. It is about understanding what people are trying to accomplish and creating the most helpful page for that need. The right topic can bring relevant visitors; the wrong page can attract traffic that never becomes a customer.

Begin with customer questions

List the questions people ask before contacting or buying: what the service includes, who it suits, how the process works, what affects cost and what alternatives exist. Speak with sales and support teams, review enquiry notes and use customer language rather than internal jargon.

Understand the intent behind a search

Informational searches seek explanations, commercial investigation compares options, transactional searches signal readiness to act, and navigational searches look for a specific brand or page. These categories overlap, so inspect the actual search results and ask what format best satisfies the query.

Group related terms into topic clusters

Organise close variations and related questions around one central topic. A single strong page may answer several closely related phrases. Create a separate page only when the intent or information required is meaningfully different.

Map one primary purpose to each page

Choose a clear primary topic for every important page and support it naturally with related language. Avoid creating multiple pages that compete to answer the same query. Use internal links to connect service pages with relevant guides and FAQs.

Prioritise by business relevance

Consider relevance to your services, customer value, competition, ability to create a better resource and the current strength of your website. Search volume estimates are directional, not promises of traffic or sales.

Turn research into a content plan

Record the target audience, intent, page type, primary topic, supporting questions, internal links and conversion action. Revisit the plan as customer needs and search results change. See VOIZON's SEO service for support with search planning.

A practical keyword mapping worksheet

For each target page, record the audience, problem, primary query theme, supporting questions, search intent, page format, conversion action and internal links. Check whether another page already serves the same intent. This simple map reduces overlap and helps writers know what a page must answer.

Use search results as a research signal

Review the types of pages appearing for a query: guides, local listings, product pages, videos or comparisons. This helps identify the expected format, but do not copy competitors. Look for unanswered customer questions and add original explanations or examples based on the business's actual expertise.

Avoid keyword cannibalisation

If two pages target the same query and answer the same question, users and search engines may struggle to understand which is the best destination. Give pages distinct purposes, combine overlapping content when appropriate and link to the preferred resource.

Frequently asked question

How many keywords should one page target?

A page can naturally cover a main topic and related questions. Focus on satisfying one coherent intent rather than assigning a separate page to every slight wording variation.
