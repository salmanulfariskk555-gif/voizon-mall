Business Website Development: A Practical Checklist Before You Hire an Agency

A practical guide to planning a business website, comparing agencies, defining scope, budgeting and preparing for launch.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · 7+ Years of Agency Experience

A business website is more than a collection of pages. It should explain what the organisation does, help the right visitors find relevant information and make the next step clear. Before hiring a web development agency, use this checklist to reduce uncertainty and compare proposals fairly.

1. Define the website's business purpose

Start by identifying the primary job of the website. Is it meant to generate enquiries, explain professional services, sell products, support existing customers, or present a company to potential partners? A site can support several goals, but prioritising them helps determine the structure, content and features.

2. Identify the audience and required pages

List the audiences who will use the site and the questions each group needs answered. A typical service-business website may need Home, About, Services, individual service pages, Portfolio or Case Studies, FAQs and Contact. The final sitemap should follow the customer's decision journey rather than an arbitrary page count.

3. Separate essential features from optional features

Write down what the website must do: enquiry forms, WhatsApp contact, booking, payment, customer login, product catalogue, multilingual content or integrations. Mark each item as essential, useful or future-phase. This makes proposals easier to compare and prevents hidden assumptions.

4. Ask what is included in the quotation

A clear proposal should state page count, design approach, content responsibilities, revisions, responsive checks, integrations, testing, launch support and handover. Confirm whether domain, hosting, paid plugins, stock assets, email services and ongoing maintenance are included or charged separately.

5. Clarify ownership and access

The business should understand who owns the domain, hosting account, website files, CMS administrator access, licences and third-party accounts. Ownership, access and handover arrangements should be written into the agreement. Do not rely on informal verbal promises.

6. Plan for search visibility from the beginning

A technically sound website should have descriptive page titles, useful page content, clear headings, crawlable links, sensible URL structures, mobile-friendly layouts, image alt text and a sitemap. Ongoing SEO research, content production and link outreach are separate activities unless specified in the scope.

7. Prepare content and approvals

Gather accurate service descriptions, company details, approved images, contact information, brand assets and policy text. Assign one person to consolidate feedback and approvals. Delayed content and fragmented feedback are common causes of schedule changes.

8. Test before launch

Test navigation, forms, phone links, WhatsApp links, mobile layouts, page speed, spelling, redirects, analytics and important conversion paths. Confirm backups, security responsibilities and who handles post-launch fixes.

How to compare website development agencies

Compare the clarity of the scope, relevant examples, technical approach, communication, ownership terms and support plan—not just the lowest price. Ask the agency to explain how its proposed solution addresses your specific business goals.
