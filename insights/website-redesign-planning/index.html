Website Redesign Planning: Protect SEO While Improving User Experience

Plan a website redesign with content inventory, URL mapping, redirects, analytics, accessibility and launch testing.

VOIZON Digital Solutions · Updated 2026-11-17

A redesign can improve clarity and usability, but it can also disrupt search visibility if important URLs, content and redirects are overlooked. Treat the redesign as both a design project and a migration that needs careful planning.

Audit the current website

List important URLs, traffic sources, conversions, backlinks, content assets and technical issues. Identify pages that perform well and understand why they matter before deciding what to remove or combine.

Define the new user journeys

Map the key audiences, questions and actions. Build navigation around the way visitors evaluate services. Keep important information easy to find and avoid unnecessary menu complexity.

Create a URL migration map

For every URL that changes, identify the closest relevant destination. Use appropriate permanent redirects for moved pages, update internal links and avoid redirecting unrelated pages to the homepage.

Preserve useful content and metadata

Review titles, descriptions, headings, canonical tags, image alt text and structured data. Improve content where necessary, but do not remove useful information simply to make the page shorter.

Test before and after launch

Check mobile layouts, forms, navigation, tracking, robots directives, sitemap, canonical URLs, redirects and important page responses. Crawl the new site and compare it with the old URL list.

Monitor the migration

After launch, monitor Search Console indexing, organic landing pages, enquiries and server errors. Investigate sustained changes and fix technical problems quickly. A redesign cannot guarantee ranking gains; careful migration reduces avoidable risk.

Create a redesign brief and acceptance checklist

Document current problems, target audiences, priority pages, required features, integrations, accessibility expectations, content responsibilities, budget and launch date. Define measurable acceptance criteria such as forms working, key pages migrated, redirects tested and analytics verified.

Preserve search equity during a redesign

Inventory existing URLs, metadata, internal links and important landing pages. Keep URLs where practical. Map moved pages to relevant destinations with permanent redirects, preserve useful content and check canonical tags, robots directives and sitemap entries before launch.

Plan content and stakeholder approvals

Identify who supplies copy, images, product details, legal text and final approvals. Obtain permission for customer photos and testimonials. Avoid publishing placeholder claims or invented portfolio work. Include time for content review in the project schedule.

Frequently asked question

What is the biggest redesign risk?

Launching a new design without a migration and testing plan can break URLs, forms, tracking or important user journeys. Treat SEO, analytics and conversion checks as part of the redesign scope.
